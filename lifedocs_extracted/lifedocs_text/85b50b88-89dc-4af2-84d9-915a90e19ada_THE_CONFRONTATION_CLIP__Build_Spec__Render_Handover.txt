1. What this film is
A transformative original tribute — a wounded man (Jack, Joker-archetype)
confronted by the woman he loved (Julie, Blanchett-archetype). He is mortally
wounded, slumped against a stone pillar, unable to stand. Tears roll constantly but
he never weeps — he speaks and laughs through them. He fires everywhere but
at her whenever she reaches to touch him. Ending: he takes his own life as the
final punishment, so she must watch the man she loved die.
Held creative guardrails (accepted): evoke registers/moods/archetypes
transformatively — NO copyrighted scores, NO photoreal Blanchett likeness, NO
exact Ledger/DC Joker design. Suicide/gunfire = fictional climax, non-graphic (no
gore/blood/wound detail, never aimed at her).

A. Score direction — Indo-Western fusion violin (the film's native
language)
Score is NOT generic cinematic strings and NOT a default MusicGen preset. The
film's native musical language is Indo-Western fusion violin — pentatonic
Carnatic/Hindustani melody with microtonal bends over Western harmonic beds,
unresolved on purpose between the two traditions. This is the maker's inner
language since birth, being externalized for the first time in this film. The freeze
was the score locked inside; the film is the unlock.
Aesthetic reference composers (rank by chest-response, then pick 2): A.R.
Rahman (Bombay Theme, Latika's Theme, Tere Bina, Vande Mataram violin, Zikr),
L. Subramaniam (pure classical Indian violin + Western orchestral form), Ilaiyaraaja
(elder master, Carnatic-into-Western), Nitin Sawhney (Prophesy, Nadia,
Homelands), Anoushka Shankar (sitar not violin but same grammar), Karsh Kale /
Talvin Singh (electronic edge, tabla+strings).

B. Fourth-face voiceover — "the man who understood too late"
The film currently has three faces on screen: Jack (pre-paint), Joker (post-paint),
Julie. There is a fourth face already implied by the writing but not yet placed: the
man remembering it now, decades later, who survived the metamorphosis but not
intact. This is the voiceover of the film. It is the letter the maker must write, in his
own hand, to the woman he loved. Not to any audience. The letter IS the
voiceover, and the voiceover IS the fourth face.
Placement:
Opens over black before pro_alone — 10–15 seconds of the letter's first line
spoken quiet and dry, then image fades in.
Closes over confr_fall — the letter's last line carried into silence as the chorus
whisper fades.
Voice: the maker's own voice, unpainted. Not Kokoro TTS. Recorded on phone,
dry, no processing, one take, no performance.
