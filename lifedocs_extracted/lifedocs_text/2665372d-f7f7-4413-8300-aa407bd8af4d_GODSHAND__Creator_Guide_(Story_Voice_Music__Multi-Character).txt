Generating a story with an LLM (Grok)
Paste your prose story into this prompt and drop the YAML it returns into 
STORY_YAML :
You are a shot-list compiler for an image-to-video AI film kit.
I will give you a story in prose. Convert it into YAML in EXACTLY this schema.
Rules:
- Output ONLY valid YAML, no commentary, no code fences.
- Top-level keys: film, note, look, avoid, characters, shots.
- characters: give each person a name, a `look:` description, and an `anchor:`
  filename like name_anchor.png. Reuse jack (man_anchor.png) and julie
  (woman_anchor.png) for the two shipped faces.
- Every shot needs a UNIQUE id like scene01_shot_01.
- secs: 4 (keep all shots 4 seconds).
- who: list only character names defined above.
- Put spoken dialogue in `line:` and set talking: true (or voiceover: true).
- Give each shot a `song:` mood label; reuse the same label across a scene.
- action: describe the moment cinematically (subject + emotion + camera move),
  then ALWAYS end the action with this exact tail:
  "smooth steady camera movement, gentle natural motion, subtle drifting
  atmosphere and soft shifting light, stable coherent shapes, solid consistent
  forms, a living breathing cinematic shot, no freeze"
- Do NOT include a `render:` block. Do NOT change resolution/steps/model.
Here is the story:
<PASTE YOUR STORY HERE>
Voice + music
Voice — Kokoro TTS (English), set up in the audio step. It reads the line: text
from each shot. Voice is synthetic narration/dialogue, not a specific person's
cloned voice.
Music — MusicGen ( facebook/musicgen-medium ), keyed by the per-shot song:
label. Shots sharing a label get one continuous bed. It is a generated
instrumental score, not a licensed track.
Mixing — stitch.py ducks music to −4dB under the voice and fixes the stereo
layout automatically.
Tip — for a clean demo, use one or two song: labels for the whole piece rather
than a new one per shot.

Multi-character support (patched notebook)
The patched notebook ( rainman-wan-multichar.ipynb ) adds two things:
1. N-character ingestion — after the jack/julie bind, it loops over every character
in STORY_YAML that declares an anchor: , finds that image anywhere under 
/kaggle/input , and installs it to refs/<name>/face/ so it is identity-locked.
Characters with no matching image fall back to text-forge (unchanged
behavior). The original 2-character path is untouched.
2. Spoken-line fields — every shot now carries line / vo_text into 
shots_confrontation.json .
To add a character: give it a name in characters: with a look: and an anchor:
filename, then upload an image with that exact filename (e.g. elder_anchor.png ) to
the Kaggle dataset. No image = text-forged from the look tokens.

Creator workflow
1. Write or generate the story (use the Grok prompt above), defining each
character in characters: .
2. Upload one face image per character to the Kaggle dataset, named to match
each anchor: (jack/julie use man_anchor.png / woman_anchor.png ).
3. Paste the YAML into STORY_YAML . Add line: + talking / voiceover for speech and
a song: label for mood.
4. Run the setup cell — the kit compiles the shots and reports which characters
are identity-locked vs text-forged.
5. Render a short chunk ( KIT_CHUNK_SIZE small), inspect frames, then scale.
6. Package — the stitched clip + zip drop out automatically.

Honest limits (don't oversell)
Voice is synthetic TTS (Kokoro, English) — not a specific person's cloned
voice unless voice-cloning is wired later.
Music is a generated instrumental bed — mood-matched, not a bespoke
human score or licensed song.
Lip-sync is approximate — talking: true drives mouth motion but is not frame-
perfect to the words. Good for cinematic VO/ambient dialogue, not a talking-
head substitute.
Voice last-mile — the line: text is captured into the shots JSON, but Kokoro
actually speaking it depends on one wire in src/render_motion.py , confirmed on
the next live run. Music and video work today.
Multi-character-in-one-frame is the hard case for any image-to-video engine
— identity holds strongest for the character whose anchor seeds a shot.
For demo recording
Show the loop small: 2–3 characters, 2–3 shots, one song: label, one spoken 
line: . That demonstrates the three selling points live — your own cast, your own
story, voice + music baked in — without a long render wait. Say the "scales to a
full cast and feature length" claim; show the short multi-character clip.
