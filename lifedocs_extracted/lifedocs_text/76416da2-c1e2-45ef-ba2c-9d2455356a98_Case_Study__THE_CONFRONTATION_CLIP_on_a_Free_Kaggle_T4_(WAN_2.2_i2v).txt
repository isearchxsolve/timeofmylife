🎯A before/after engineering case study of rendering a 26-shot cinematic
clip on a free dual-T4 Kaggle notebook using WAN 2.2 I2V. The headline:
the GPU was never the bottleneck — a single misconfigured
postprocess line was, and fixing it cut the projected render from ~33 h
to ~5–6 h at $0 cost.
1. Initial observations from the output folders (creator's
field notes)
These are the firsthand observations from inspecting the actual output folders of
that run — the raw signal that seeded the whole analysis below.
👁️Observation 1 — the raw beats the final.
take_0 (the raw WAN output) is
visibly sharper and higher quality than the delivered final.mp4 . The
postprocess chain is subtracting quality, not adding it.
Observation 2 — no real motion. There is no actual motion beyond the
original gesture already posed in the source image. The clip animates
the pose it was handed and little else — the shot is effectively a living
still.
Observation 3 — costume held. Across the whole clip the character
kept the same costume — identity/wardrobe consistency was preserved
end-to-end, confirming the stills anchor is doing its job on the 
consistency axis even while motion stays flat.

===== PAGE 2/6 =====
The creator's architecture & proposition (in their own words)
The observations above converged into an explicit design thesis, stated firsthand
during the session. Reproduced verbatim, then unpacked:
💬"Why not allow WAN model to exercise full freedom — rest of quality
and other hacks we do offline using well-established image and video
processing algorithms."
"Consistency can be interpolated across frames. Let WAN do the heavy
lifting — the actual generation… I think going by the initial stills
generation [is] the key professional cinema approach."
Unpacked, this is a keyframe-driven generative-film architecture — and it maps
1:1 onto the professional order of operations (storyboard → key art → animate →
finish):
1. The initial stills are the key. The frame is the unit of craft: composition,
identity, costume, and lighting are all baked into the anchor still. It's also where
quality-per-compute is highest — fixing one frame is cheap and deterministic;
fixing 41 moving frames is not. Front-load quality into the still and the whole
shot inherits it. (This is the strongest insight — it's ~80% of the battle.)
2. Let WAN exercise full freedom on motion. Anchor on the still, then let the
model generatively interpolate the in-betweens instead of clamping it to a
living photo. WAN owns the temporal axis; the still only sets the spatial ceiling.
3. Do the rest of the quality work offline, with established algorithms. Grade,
denoise, upscale as a finishing pass after download — off the GPU quota,
using well-understood image/video tooling. Finishing, never rescue.
4. "Consistency can be interpolated" — but only the right kind. Generative
interpolation (WAN creating semantically coherent in-between frames) is the
real thing and builds consistency within a shot. Optical interpolation (RIFE /
ffmpeg minterpolate ) does not create consistency — it blends pixels and
amplifies artifacts, which is exactly what degraded final.mp4 . Across hard cuts
nothing interpolates at all — which is why consistent stills are the foundation.

===== PAGE 3/6 =====
✅"Is this architecture already implemented?" — Yes, verified in the kit
source. The default render mode is the blueprint path
( KIT_RENDER_MODE=motion_stills : "stills first, then animate each still into
motion"); stills are a dedicated first-class stage; cross-shot flf2v
chaining exists; identities are forged up front. The creator
independently reverse-engineered the exact architecture the kit
already ships. The catch: it's the default tuning that undercut it —
motion denoise defaulted to 0.35 , end-frame morph was OFF by default
(hence the frozen poses), the stills stage ran at economy settings, and
the lossy CPU post (incl. minterpolate ) was on. So the fix is parameter
tuning, not a rebuild — a config problem sitting on top of a sound
design.

6. Quality tradeoffs
take_0 (raw) looked sharper than final (postprocessed). The CPU upscale +
grade pass was softening detail rather than adding it. → Skip per-shot spatial
upscale; do a single HD upscale on CPU after download (zero GPU quota).
Motion was "stuck to pose." WAN I2V anchored too tightly to the forged start
frame → static shots. → Lower conditioning fps and/or raise denoise so WAN
does more of the motion "heavy lifting."
Interpolate once, not per shot. Deliver at native 16 fps per shot; do any
smooth-motion interpolation once at final assembly, not 26 times mid-pipeline.

8. The bigger picture
The film without the architecture story is forgettable. The architecture without
the film is unconvincing. Together they're a case study.
What this project actually demonstrates: a full multi-character, scored, voiced
cinematic pipeline running end-to-end on free consumer-tier cloud GPUs, with
identity invented from prompts (no photos), resumable across sessions, and gated
so it can never overspend. The "means of production" for short AI film are now
effectively free to anyone who shows up with the engineering discipline to tune
the pipeline — which is exactly what the before/after above proves.
