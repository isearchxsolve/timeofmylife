🫥The horror here is not blood — it's atmosphere. Kindness worn as a
mask. Danger in full daylight. Empty roads, a distant siren, no one to
witness. That restraint is what makes it hit hard and look premium.
Logline
A bright, silent afternoon on a deserted road. A lone figure. Strangers approach
wearing warm, easy smiles — and something in the air goes cold. They come
close, they take what they came for, and they leave without a word, without a
promise, without ever looking back. The warmth was the weapon.
Master scene prompt (for Flow / Veo)
Cinematic thriller, harsh midday sun, a silent empty small-town road like a
desert — not a soul in sight. A lone figure stands still. From the shimmering
heat-haze, two or three strangers approach unhurried, wearing warm friendly
smiles that never reach their eyes. The light is bright but the mood is ice. A faint
distant siren wails somewhere far off, unanswered. The strangers draw close,
calm and pleasant, then the warmth curdles — a flicker of something cold and
predatory beneath the smile. Muted bleached color palette, deep shadows
under a white sky, 35mm anamorphic, shallow depth of field, unsettling
stillness. Static locked-off camera — no zoom, no push-in, no camera
movement whatsoever. No dialogue. Dread in broad daylight.

The background score
Do NOT put music over the whole scene. That kills the dread. Music should
appear in only 2 places:
1. Shot 3–4 (the approach + the cold flicker): a single low sub-bass drone —
not a melody, just a frequency you feel in your chest. It swells slowly as the
strangers approach, peaks when the smile holds too long, then cuts to dead
silence on the turn-away in shot 5.
2. Shot 6 (after): a single sustained cello note — low, unresolved, fading —
enters under the end-card text and dies out before the video ends. One note.
Not a melody. Just the sound of something that will never be answered.
How to do it in CapCut (free)
Silence: don't add a track. Just let the clips play dry. The absence of sound IS
the sound design.
Sub-bass drone: search CapCut's audio library for "dark ambient" or "drone"
— pick the lowest, simplest one. Trim it to shots 3–4 only. Fade in slow, cut
hard.
Cello note: search "cello" or "strings" — pick a single sustained low note. Trim
to shot 6. Fade out before the video ends.

The golden rule
Less sound = more dread. Every second of silence in this scene is doing more
work than any track could. When in doubt, cut it out.

Color & tone
Bleached, overexposed whites; the sun as a threat, not a comfort.
Deep hard shadows. High contrast. Slightly desaturated.
The wrongness is the contrast: warm light, cold heart.

The lone figure (save this in Flow's Characters tab)
An ordinary man in his mid-30s, lean and tired, short dark hair slightly unkempt,
light stubble, brown eyes, wearing a faded olive-green jacket over a plain white

🎬Same as your reel: turn each shot-prompt into a clip, then stitch. One
step at a time — don't rush it.
1. Open Flow. Save your lone figure in the Characters tab so the face stays the
same across shots.
2. Generate the 6 shots one by one. For each, paste the master scene prompt
(the mood paragraph) + that shot's line from the table.
3. On shot 5, remember: cut away before contact. Never show it.
4. Open CapCut. Drop the 6 clips in order (1 → 6).
5. Add the sound: near silence, a faint far-off siren, a low drone that swells —
then hard silence on the turn-away in shot 5.
6. Add the end-card text at the finish.
7. Export. Done.
